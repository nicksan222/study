# Carbon cycle and ecosystem change

Carbon reservoirs include the atmosphere, living biomass, soils, oceans and geological deposits. A reservoir is a stock, measured as an amount. Photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion and exchange with oceans are fluxes, measured as amounts per unit time.

## Stock and flow

A reservoir grows when its total inputs exceed its outputs over the period considered. A large stock does not imply a large annual flux. Always state both the system boundary and the time interval when comparing estimates.

## Ecosystem processes

Photosynthesis incorporates carbon into organic matter. Organisms use some of that material in respiration. Dead material enters decomposition pathways, while disturbance can rapidly release part of a stored pool. Rates depend on environmental conditions and the organisms involved.

## Land cover

Changes in vegetation, soil management and impervious surfaces affect carbon storage, habitat structure and water movement. A local intervention can therefore have several outcomes, and a single indicator cannot describe the entire ecosystem.

## Observation and uncertainty

Field surveys measure selected places and times with imperfect detection. Record the method and effort, retain uncertain identifications, and distinguish a missing observation from demonstrated absence.
