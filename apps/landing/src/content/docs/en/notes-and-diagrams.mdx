Capture while you learn; revise when you need a clearer account. [Sessions](../sessions/) hold the working notes. [Documents](../documents/) give you a place to organize the result.

## Choose the change you need

A transformation can improve clarity or grammar, organize the structure, remove repetition, summarize, explain, expand or translate. Add your own instructions when the goal is more specific.

A shorter summary is useful for a quick revision. A structured explanation is useful when the connections matter. Choose the operation for the job, rather than asking every transformation to do everything.

## Combine related material

Use **From sessions** in Documents to bring several saved sessions into one document. Select the relevant sessions and sources, then explain what should be combined. You can also merge existing documents.

Save unsent notes before starting. Review the selection and the result: long material is subject to the transformation's context limit.

## Review before relying on it

Read proposed factual corrections against your original sources. Keep important qualifications and disagreements rather than accepting smoother wording as proof of accuracy.

Use the editor for your own changes and the version controls to revisit earlier text. Reading an earlier version and making it current are separate actions.

## Use the revised result

Choose the resulting document when [generating practice](../practice/) or [flashcards](../flashcards/). The selected version provides the material for that generation. Revise first, then generate a new set when you want your changes included.
