Search looks through everything you've put into Study: projects, sessions, your files and what's inside them, and your messages. It matches the words you type and, once its model is installed, what they mean.

## Open search

Press Ctrl K, or Cmd K on macOS, from anywhere in the app. A box opens that says **Search projects, sessions, and files…**. Start typing; results appear as you go.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| ↑ and ↓ | Move through the results |
| Enter | Open the selected result |
| Esc | Close search |

![Search opens with Ctrl K, and results appear and narrow as a word is typed.](clip:search-typing "Results appear as you type.")

## What you get back

Results come in groups:

- **Projects**, by name
- **Sessions**, by title
- **Files**, by name
- **In your files**, passages inside your files that match
- **Messages**, your notes and the answers in your sessions

Opening a file or a passage shows that file: beside its session if it was attached to one, otherwise in the Library. A message opens its session.

## Words and meaning

Study searches in two ways at once and combines the results.

- **By words**, it finds passages that contain what you typed. This works from the start.
- **By meaning**, it finds passages about the same thing even when they use other words, or another language. A search for "cell energy" can find a passage about mitochondria and ATP.

Search by meaning needs a small model that runs on your computer. It's a multilingual model of about 135 MB, and it never sends your text anywhere. Download it in **Settings › This computer**. Until you do, search shows **Not installed: search matches words only**, and everything else keeps working.

After you install the model, Study prepares your existing files in the background. While it does, search says **Preparing search by meaning; showing exact matches for now.** New files are prepared as they're read. You can follow the work in [Activity](../background-work/), under **Search by meaning**.

## What gets searched

A file shows up inside **In your files** once Study has read it, which for a recording means once it's transcribed. Files Study can't read are still found by name. See [sources](../sources/) for which kinds are read.

Each kind of source can be left out of the search index, or of search by meaning, in **Settings › Processing** under **Search**.

If a file's text is wrong, fix it with **Correct this text** in **File details**. Search uses your correction.

## Search and answers

[Answers](../answers/) use the same index to find related passages across a project. A source that search can find well is one that answers can draw on well, so installing the search model also helps @study find what you mean.
